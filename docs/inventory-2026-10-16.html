Pages, and what search engines see

Two numbers matter here and they are different from each other.

Ten addresses are indexable. The home page and the nine trade pages each live at their own URL, with their own tab title and their own meta description written for the search term that trade is looking for. Those are the pages Google can rank separately.

Nine more pages are part of the home document. Who we are, Our Promise, Our Projects, FAQ, For Realtors, Become a Partner, Franchises, Privacy Policy and Terms of Service are each a full page to a visitor, with their own heading, their own content and their own place in the menu. Technically they are reached by a fragment of the home address, so a search engine treats them as part of the home page rather than as separate results. That is a deliberate build decision and it can be changed, but it is worth knowing before the SEO work starts.

Every one of the nineteen exists in English and in French. The two dictionaries were checked against each other line by line: 494 entries, the same list in both, nothing missing on either side and nothing left in English on a French page.

Not done yet, by agreement

Search optimisation was set aside until the site is finished and the GoDaddy domain is authorised. Two things will need attention then: the sitemap currently lists four addresses and should list all ten, including the nine trade pages, and the site carries no structured data, which is what puts a local trades business into map results and rich listings.

Work done on the photographs

  • All galleries were recropped to square. They arrived from a phone at nine by sixteen, which left a tall column of empty space beside the text on every trade page. Each crop was checked by eye first, so nothing loses its subject.
  • Every image was converted to WebP at 800 pixels wide, which is what keeps 158 images down to 38 MB.
  • Orientation was corrected where the camera had recorded it in the file rather than the pixels, which is what makes phone photographs appear on their side.
  • Two faults were found and fixed on the way: one before and after pair had been loaded the wrong way round, and two staging images turned out to be byte for byte identical.
  • One supplied photograph carried another company's branding, legible on a jacket. It was cropped down to the sign alone, and the full frame still needs a replacement photograph.

Video

Eight finished clips with a still frame each, 36 MB in total. Every one was edited, re-encoded for the web and stripped of its audio track.

The four crew films

Cleaning, Decluttering, Curb Appeal and Flooring each carry a film of the crew on site. None of these existed as a single clip. Each was built from two separate vertical phone recordings, placed side by side into one frame so the pair fills the space exactly with no black bars and nothing invented to fill the gaps. Each runs thirty seconds and loops without a visible join.

The flooring pair needed extra work: the two recordings had been shot at different heights, 1080 by 1920 against 1080 by 2320, so both were cropped to a common frame before they could sit together.

The home page, and a note about the two clips there

The home page opens with two videos playing one after the other, fading from one into the next rather than cutting. Neither carries sound. The drone flight opens, and the renovation sequence follows it.

Worth knowing about the home page video

The opening clip is the real drone flight over the job. The original recording is excellent, 2560 by 1440 and sharp. The copy on the website is 1280 by 720, and it looks a little softer than the clip that follows it. The reason is the footage itself: thirty four seconds of continuous aerial movement over dense trees and mulch is the hardest thing there is to compress, because almost every pixel changes in every frame. It was first published at 960 by 540, which looked noticeably poor, and has since been re-encoded at double that resolution. At 10.5 MB it is the heaviest file on the site.

The second clip is the renovation sequence, created with AI. It is fifteen seconds at 1280 by 720 and it looks clean, because generated footage of this kind compresses easily. It is not downloaded at all until the drone clip is nearly finished, so it costs the visitor nothing on arrival.

One improvement is worth considering now that the drone clip opens the page. Shortening it from thirty four seconds to about fifteen would roughly halve its weight and sharpen it at the same time, because the quality budget would be spread over half as much footage. It would also mean the home page starts with a 5 MB file instead of a 10.5 MB one, which matters most to a visitor arriving on mobile data. Nobody watches a looping background video for thirty four seconds. It needs a decision on which part of the flight to keep.

Forms and the enquiries they produce

Four separate forms, each writing to the same place.

The free quote form is a step by step wizard that asks what needs doing, the type of property, its size, the areas involved and the work wanted, then takes contact details and consent. The other three are shorter and sit on the page they belong to: the agent form on For Realtors, the trade form on Become a Partner, and the territory form on Franchises. Each page shows its own form on the floating button rather than the generic quote form.

All four were tested end to end and each sends the correct type with the fields the administration page reads. Enquiries arrive in a private administration area where each one can be opened, given a status and worked through.
